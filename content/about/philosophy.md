<details name="philosophy">
<summary>🎮 Life is a Sandbox</summary>

As a society we obsess over success too much.<br>What we call "success" is often survivor bias with extra iteration steps.

Most choices are experiments, not judgments.<br>
Most values are made, not inherited.

So try things. Keep what works. Respec when it doesn't.

<br>

</details>

<details name="philosophy">
<summary>⚙️ People are Systems</summary>

Behavior rarely appears from nowhere.<br>We're more like state machines than mystic soups.

Everyone is programmed by feedback loops and adaptations.

Understanding the mechanism is more useful than attaching a label.

<br>

</details>

<details name="philosophy">
<summary>🗺️ Worldviews are Models</summary>

We assume too easily that other people are working from the same map.<br>We also assume too easily that our own map is reality and that it's obvious.

What I value is the ability to distance yourself from your initial assumptions,<br>
look at the model from the outside, and update it when reality disagrees.

<br>

</details>

<details name="philosophy">
<summary>🧭 Make Meaning, Don’t Wait for It</summary>

I don't think life needs a built-in cosmic objective to be worth playing.<br>I find "what is the meaning of life?" a strangely loaded question,<br>because it assumes there should be one canonical answer.

Meaning can be local, temporary and completely invented:<br>
a person, a project, curiosity, a stupid game you really want to finish.

Manufactured meaning still works, sometimes better than inherited.

<br>

</details>

<details name="philosophy">
<summary>🎯 Choose Agency</summary>

Obsessing over what you can't control isn't helpful.<br>We tend to complain about things that are out of our reach.

Agency doesn't mean control.<br>
It means looking for the part of the system that still responds to input.<br>Excuses consume the same bandwidth you could spend looking for leverage.

<br>

</details>

<details name="philosophy">
<summary>🪞 Epistemic Humility is the Key</summary>

People are contradictory, biased and awfully illogical.<br>
We're all running on incomplete information, biases and imperfect models.

So we should never antagonise anyone or reduce them to labels.<br>We're just in different states, observing and judging each other.<br>Sometimes distance is still the healthiest solution, though.

Self-awareness, humility and the willingness to improve are much more useful<br>than polishing our own worldview and cherry-picking to sustain it.

<br>

</details>

<details name="philosophy">
<summary>🧬 Design Around Human Nature</summary>

People are bad at consistently doing what they "should".<br>We're much more impulsive and emotional than we want to admit.

I believe convenience is the main drive of behavior.<br>And that acceptance is paradoxically the main drive of personal change.

So instead of demanding perfect discipline,<br>I prefer changing the environment, incentives and defaults.

Good systems don't require ideal humans.<br>
They are designed around their flaws.

</details>
